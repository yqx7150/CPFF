Lensless imaging replaces traditional optical systems with planar encoding masks, offering significant advantages in terms of miniaturization and cost. Multi-phase Fresnel zone aperture (FZA) encoded lensless imaging effectively suppresses twin-image artifacts in reconstructed images. However, integrating multiple masks on a single substrate inevitably leads to field-of-view compression, while physical constraints such as sensor size may result in finite sampling issues. To address the challenges in image reconstruction, a coding domain prior aided FZA lensless imaging method under finite sampling (CPFF) is proposed. Specifically, a high-dimensional tensor is constructed from dual-phase FZA encoded images to directly learn the prior distribution of the encoded light field. Based on the physical propagation model of FZA, image reconstruction is achieved through alternating iterations between the coding domain and the spatial domain. The learned coding domain prior is utilized to constrain the recovery of unsampled data, while the spatial domain prior is employed to remove artifacts in the reconstructed image. Simulation and experimental results demonstrate the superior image quality of CPFF over comparative algorithms under finite sampling. The robustness of CPFF is further confirmed through cross-dataset generalization validation and experiments with various sensor sampling configurations.

## Flowchart of CPFF. Prior learning: The coding domain diffusion model is trained to capture the correlative prior information between dual-phase encoded images, while the spatial domain diffusion model is trained to learn the global detail priors of twin-image-free targets. Image reconstruction: Based on the physical propagation model of FZA, alternating iterations are performed between the coding and spatial domains. The predictor-corrector sampler is embedded within the iterative loop, incorporating measurement data fidelity to achieve high-quality image restoration.
